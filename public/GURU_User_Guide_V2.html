1 — Introduction

G.U.R.U. is an AI-powered doctoral research intelligence system designed specifically for PhD candidates in law and cognate disciplines. It combines a large-language-model supervisor with simultaneous access to 22 academic and legal databases, a full reference management system, oral examination preparation, argument mapping, and supervisor reporting — all within a single browser-based application requiring no installation.

GURU is not a general-purpose chatbot. It is calibrated to your specific research directive, speaks in the register of a senior legal practitioner and academic, applies OSCOLA 4th Edition throughout, and builds an understanding of your research that deepens over each session.

Core principle
GURU is designed to function as three things simultaneously: a legal practitioner who understands the law in context, a legal academic who knows the scholarly terrain, and a domain expert in the scientific or technical field underpinning your research.

2 — Quick Start

  1. Get a free Gemini API key from aistudio.google.com/apikey. No payment required.
  2. Open GURU and navigate to the Config tab.
  3. Paste your key into Gemini API Key and press Save & Calibrate.
  4. Write your PhD Research Focus — be specific: "Medical negligence and genomic testing under UK tort law, focusing on CRISPR liability, duty of disclosure, and rights-based compensation frameworks. PhD at University of Brighton."
  5. Return to Dialectic and ask your first research question. GURU is now calibrated to your field.
Optional: Claude API
GURU also supports Anthropic's Claude models. Add a Claude API key in Config to switch providers, or use Claude for specific modes while keeping Gemini for others.

4 — Dialectic

The Dialectic tab is the primary research interface. Every query is processed against your research directive, your Reference Library, your Research Vault, and — when Search is active — 22 simultaneous academic and legal databases.

🎤One-shot voice input — speak your query, click again to submit.

Search — 22 databases in parallel

When 🔍 SEARCH is active, GURU queries all 22 databases simultaneously the moment you submit. Results appear progressively in the status bar as databases respond. All results are automatically saved to your Reference Library with OSCOLA citations.

How search integrates
Retrieved sources are injected directly into the AI's context for that query — GURU reads the abstracts, authors, and DOIs and cites them in its response. The sources are simultaneously saved to your Library. You do not need to do anything extra.

Voice output

Click Voice in the header to enable audio output of GURU's responses. GURU uses Gemini TTS (high-quality, gravitas voices including Charon, Fenrir, Kore) with automatic fallback to your device's speech synthesis if Gemini TTS is unavailable.

Select your preferred voice in Config → GURU Voice. Charon is recommended for doctoral supervision contexts.

The Research Relay

Neither Gemini Gems nor Claude Projects expose a public API, so GURU cannot query them directly. The Relay provides an efficient manual bridge:

  1. Ask your question in your Gemini Gem or Claude Project and copy the response.
  2. In GURU, click ⇄ RELAY.
  3. Paste the response and click Relay into GURU.
  4. GURU scans for case citations, statutes, and DOIs — saves them to your Library — and uses the full content as research context for your next query.

File attachments

Click 📎 to attach files to your query:

  • PDFs — GURU reads the full text using PDF.js. Attach thesis drafts, source articles, court judgments.
  • Images — GURU analyses diagrams, figures, screenshots, and handwritten notes.
  • Spreadsheets (.xlsx, .csv) — GURU reads the data via SheetJS and can analyse, interpret, or discuss the content.
  • Text files (.txt, .md) — Loaded directly as context.

5 — Research Library

The Library tab combines two previously separate systems into a single scrollable view: the Research Vault (uploaded documents) and the Reference Manager (bibliographic citations).

Research Vault

The Vault stores documents you upload for GURU to read and reference. Use it for:

  • PDFs of source articles you have downloaded
  • Draft thesis chapters for critique or consistency analysis
  • Session logs and research notes
  • Judgments, legislation, and policy documents

Drag and drop files into the vault drop zone, or click + Load Files. GURU can then reference, quote, and cross-cite these sources in any Dialectic conversation.

Reference Manager

The Reference Manager automatically accumulates bibliographic references from three sources:

  1. Search results — every source GURU finds via 🔍 SEARCH is saved automatically with OSCOLA formatting.
  2. AI responses — GURU scans its own responses for case citations, statute references, and DOIs and saves them.
  3. Manual entry — click + Add Reference to add sources via Paste Text, DOI Lookup, or Manual form.

Adding references manually

MethodWhen to use
Paste TextCopy a citation in any format (APA, Harvard, MLA, Chicago) from Google Scholar, a journal page, or a legal database — GURU converts it to OSCOLA automatically.

Chapter organisation

Each reference card includes a chapter selector. Assign any reference to a thesis chapter (Introduction, Literature Review, Ch1–5, Methodology, Conclusion) and chapter filter pills appear above the reference list. Switch between chapters to view only the sources relevant to each section.

Reading notes

Each reference card has a notes textarea for reading annotations, quoted passages, page references, and critical observations. Notes are saved permanently and incorporated into GURU's research context when discussing that source.

Gap Analysis

Click 📊 Gap Analysis in the Library header. GURU analyses your entire reference list against your research directive and returns a structured audit identifying: strengths, gaps by jurisdiction and time period, source type imbalances, and five specific search recommendations.

Exporting references

  • Refs .rtf — Word-processor-compatible bibliography export with Table of Cases, Table of Legislation, and other sources formatted in OSCOLA.
  • Refs RIS — Universal reference format compatible with Zotero, Mendeley, EndNote, and RefWorks.

Zotero integration

  1. Create a free account at zotero.org.
  2. Go to zotero.org/settings/keys — note your User ID and create a new API key with write access.
  3. Paste both into Config → Zotero Integration and save.
  4. Click ⇄ Zotero in the Library header to push your entire reference list to Zotero in one operation.

Once in Zotero, use their PDF reader for full annotation, highlighting, and note-taking. Relay annotated notes back into GURU via the ⇄ Relay button.

6 — Supervision Mode Reference

Supervise

The default mode. GURU acts as a doctoral supervisor — it guides your thinking, develops your arguments, identifies connections to your reference library, and responds at the level of rigour expected at PhD. Use it for general research discussion, literature questions, and developing your theoretical framework.

Critique

GURU becomes a demanding critic. It reads your passage and systematically identifies every logical weakness, unsupported claim, methodological flaw, circular argument, and gap in reasoning. It will not soften its assessment. Paste any draft passage and receive a detailed critique before submission.

Sources

GURU identifies which specific claims in your passage require citation and suggests the most relevant cases, statutes, and academic sources in OSCOLA format. Activating this mode automatically turns on 🔍 SEARCH so that real sources from 22 databases are suggested.

Consistency

GURU maps the argumentative structure of your passage and identifies internal contradictions, logical incoherence, and inconsistencies with your stated thesis position. Use it when a passage has been revised multiple times and you want to check it still holds together.

Expand

GURU takes a well-formed argument and strengthens it — suggesting additional supporting authorities, deepening the doctrinal analysis, proposing counterarguments to engage, and identifying how the argument could develop further.

Examiner

GURU adopts the perspective of an external PhD examiner. It applies doctoral examination standards: originality of contribution, critical engagement with the literature, methodological rigour, command of the field. Use this for viva preparation and stress-testing individual passages.

Draft

GURU assists with academic legal writing. It produces authoritative prose in the register of a senior barrister or legal academic — structured, formally signposted, with inline OSCOLA citations. Provide your argument in rough form and GURU will return a draft passage you can refine.

7 — Viva

The Viva tab simulates a PhD oral examination. GURU acts as an internal examiner with full knowledge of your research directive and vault contents. It questions your thesis at the level of rigour of a real viva voce.

Before you start
Upload your thesis chapters or a thesis summary to your Vault first. GURU will reference the actual content of your work during the examination — making the simulation significantly more realistic.

Three modes are available:

  • Text only — examiner questions appear in text; you type responses
  • Examiner speaks — examiner questions are spoken aloud via TTS
  • Full voice — examiner speaks; you respond via microphone

8 — Present

The Present tab generates a structured academic presentation of your research. GURU draws on your directive, vault, and reference library to produce a systematic overview suitable for presenting to supervisors, at conferences, or in seminars.

9 — Living Journal & Supervisor Reports

The Journal tab is a structured research diary that accumulates your argument maps and critical reflections over time. It serves two functions:

  • Living record — each argument map you generate in Tools can be saved to the Journal with a critical reflection. The journal builds a documented record of your intellectual progress.
  • Supervisor reports — GURU analyses your journal entries and generates a formal progress report formatted for emailing to your supervisors, covering: research progress, argument development, sources reviewed, and outstanding questions.

To email a report: add your supervisor's email address in Config → Supervisor Report. GURU pre-fills the To field when you trigger report generation.

10 — Tools

The Tools tab generates visual argument and logic maps using Mermaid diagram syntax. These are useful for mapping the structure of a legal argument, visualising relationships between cases and doctrine, or diagramming your theoretical framework.

Generated maps can be:

  • Viewed inline in GURU
  • Opened in mermaid.live for editing and export
  • Saved to your Living Journal with a critical reflection

11 — Data

The Data tab provides spreadsheet functionality for quantitative research data. Upload or create spreadsheets, edit cells, and ask GURU to analyse, interpret, or discuss the data in the context of your research.

Supported formats: .xlsx, .csv. You can also attach spreadsheets directly in Dialectic for AI analysis within a conversation.

Research Directive

The most important configuration setting. Write a specific description of your research: topic, institution, theoretical framework, jurisdiction, methodological approach. GURU uses this to calibrate every response. The directive also appears in the strip above the input bar as a persistent reminder.

Example directive
"Genomic negligence under UK tort law — specifically liability for failure to disclose pathogenic genetic variants (BRCA1/BRCA2) and CRISPR treatment risks. Rights-based approach using Article 8 ECHR. PhD at University of Brighton School of Law. Focus on duty of care, causation (loss of autonomy), and novel heads of damage."

14 — Recommended Workflows

Literature Review

1
Set research directive

Write a specific directive in Config covering your topic, jurisdiction, theoretical framework, and institution.

2
Search by concept

Enable 🔍 SEARCH in Dialectic and ask GURU about the core concepts in your research. GURU queries all 22 databases and saves results to your Library automatically.

3
Use Cited By for depth

Open the Library, find a key source, and click Cited By ↗. Save the most relevant citing papers. This maps the intellectual lineage of key authorities.

4
Tag by chapter

Use the chapter selector on each ref card to organise sources by thesis chapter. Filter by chapter to assess coverage per section.

5
Run Gap Analysis

Click 📊 Gap Analysis in the Library header. GURU identifies missing jurisdictions, source type imbalances, and recommends five priority searches.

6
Export to Zotero

Click ⇄ Zotero to push your full library to Zotero for deep PDF annotation and reading.

Viva Preparation

1
Upload thesis to Vault

Load your thesis chapters as PDFs or text files into the Research Vault. GURU will reference actual content during examination.

2
Stress-test in Examiner mode

In Dialectic, switch to Examiner mode and paste key passages. Ask GURU to question your contribution, methodology, and argument.

3
Full simulation in Viva tab

Open the Viva tab and run a full simulated oral examination with voice enabled. Practice your spoken responses under examination conditions.

4
Identify weak points

Use Critique mode on passages where GURU's Examiner mode raised questions. Understand the weakness before the real examination.

Citation Management

1
Search populates Library automatically

Enable 🔍 SEARCH — every GURU response automatically saves cited sources to your Library with OSCOLA formatting.

2
Add Google Scholar finds

In Google Scholar, click Cite under a result, copy the citation, go to Library → + Add Reference → Paste Text. GURU converts to OSCOLA.

3
Get full text

Click Full Text ↗ on any ref card with a DOI. Unpaywall finds the free legal PDF — open it directly or download and upload to your Vault.

4
Copy footnote to Word

When writing, click Copy Fn on any ref card. The OSCOLA footnote is in your clipboard — paste directly into a Word footnote field.

5
Export bibliography

Click Refs .rtf for a formatted OSCOLA bibliography, or Refs RIS to import the full library into Zotero or Mendeley.

Supervisor Report

1
Build your journal

After generating argument maps in Tools, click Save to Journal and add a critical reflection on what the map revealed about your argument.

2
Generate report

Open the Journal tab and click Generate Supervisor Report. GURU analyses your journal entries and produces a formal progress report.

3
Email to supervisors

The report is pre-addressed to the email(s) in your Config. Review, export as RTF if needed, and send.

15 — Tips & Shortcuts

  • Enter submits a query. Use Shift+Enter for a new line within the input.
  • Keep your research directive updated as your thesis evolves — it is the single most important calibration setting.
  • Use Sources mode on every draft passage before your supervisor sees it. It will flag every uncited claim.
  • The Relay button is particularly powerful when combined with a Gemini Gem you have trained on your thesis — paste its responses into GURU to merge Gem-specific knowledge with GURU's search and reference management.
  • Chapter tags on references become essential once your library exceeds 50 sources. Tag as you go, not retrospectively.
  • For case law, always check BAILII ↗ after GURU cites a UK case — BAILII is the definitive free source for the full judgment text.
  • The Gap Analysis is most useful after you have accumulated at least 30–40 references. Run it before each supervisor meeting.
  • If Gemini TTS is unavailable (API not configured or usage quota reached), GURU automatically falls back to your device's speech synthesis — voice output continues uninterrupted.
  • Cloud sync (Pro) uses a SHA-256 hash of your passphrase as the Firestore node identifier — no account or personal data is stored. Use the same passphrase on any device to access your library and chat history.
Pro licence
